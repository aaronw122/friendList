# Friend List — R2 Review Verdict (Judge)

Inputs: PLAN.md · r2-consolidated (prosecution) · r2-challenge (defense) · r1-verdict (prior adjudication).
Severity bar: "the app still delivers its core promise" (friend-sent songs auto-added, privately, set-and-forget). Silent non-function is severe. R1-settled items (M5=R1-M5, M6=R1-M7, C1=R1-M8) are not re-litigated absent genuinely new reasoning.

## Contested platform facts (judge's findings)

1. **FDA grant does not kill a running app.** Granting Full Disk Access in System Settings posts a dialog noting the app won't have the access until it quits, with an *optional* "Quit & Reopen" button. There is no involuntary termination. However, "Quit & Reopen" is the path macOS itself suggests and many users will click it — so a mid-onboarding relaunch is the *common* outcome even though it is never forced, and an already-running process generally does not see the new grant until relaunch (the Terminal case R1 cited). Defense correct on the fact; the relaunch scenario remains practically live.
2. **`SMAppService.mainApp` is login-only.** It registers the app to auto-launch at login; it carries no `KeepAlive`/crash-restart semantics. Crash-restart is `launchd` LaunchAgent territory (`SMAppService.agent` with a `KeepAlive` plist — which can point at the same bundled executable, so a watchdog would not require a second code identity). Both sides agree on the fact; they dispute the remedy.
3. **Spotify Development Mode: the app owner does NOT need to add their own account under User Management.** The owner authenticates directly; the "user not registered in the Developer Dashboard" 403 hits non-owner, un-allowlisted users. The plan's model is strictly owner==user (BYO client_id), which is exactly the case needing no self-add. Defense correct. (Spotify's dev-mode policies have been tightening through 2025–26, so the existing impl-note "verify at M2" stays — but as a verification line, not a Must-fix.)
4. **App translocation is real and consequential.** A quarantined app launched from the DMG/Downloads runs from a randomized read-only path. A translocated path breaks `SMAppService.mainApp` registration (the recorded path won't exist next login) and confuses the FDA grant flow (the user is pointed at an ephemeral path; TCC's csreq matching mitigates but does not save the login-item leg). A Finder move to /Applications clears translocation. The fact is real; the dispute is whether "user runs straight from the DMG" is an edge case — it is not, it is one of the most common macOS user behaviors.

## Per-issue verdicts

C1: DOWNGRADED (Medium — fold into R1-M8) - The dramatic premise is false: macOS does not kill or force-relaunch an app when FDA is granted (fact 1). Strip that and the residue is verbatim R1's M8, already adjudicated Medium: grant may not be visible until relaunch, so persist an onboarding-step marker and resume at Scope. R2 does add one genuinely new, real wrinkle from the menubar-only collapse: on a mid-onboarding relaunch (which *will* commonly happen via the user clicking the suggested "Quit & Reopen"), an LSUIElement app must actually re-present and activate the onboarding window (`NSApp.activate` + explicit window presentation when `!didOnboard`) — the plan's `didOnboard` flag implies but does not specify this. That is an implementation note attached to M8's Medium, not a new Critical. Severity inflation on a misread premise; the underlying work item stands at Medium.

M1: DOWNGRADED (Medium) - The platform fact is true (fact 2): a crash kills scanning until next login, and neither side pretends otherwise. But the impact is bounded in exactly the way the defense argues: the persisted watermark makes a crash a *delay*, never a *loss* — the scanner catches up from `last_seen` at next login. Crash-death is probabilistic (requires a bug), unlike R1's upheld Must-fixes which were deterministic designed-in failures (token rotation). It does not meet the bar alone. Two requirements attach to the Medium: (a) the plan must explicitly choose and document — either accept login-only recovery in writing, or add the cheap `SMAppService.agent` KeepAlive watchdog pointing at the same bundled executable (no second code identity, contra the defense's over-engineering framing); and (b) M4 acceptance gains a crash-recovery line. Note the interaction with M4 below: an unapproved login item removes even the "next login" recovery — see Holistic.

M2: DISMISSED - The central premise is false for the plan's model (fact 3): a Spotify dev-app owner authenticating with the owning account needs no User Management self-add; the 403 the finding cites is a non-owner problem, and the plan is strictly owner==user. The prosecution's own filing betrays the weakness — agent2 filed it Low, agent3 as an impl-note "verify," and impl-note #51 literally says "confirm whether a dev-app owner must self-add" — a verification flag inflated to Must-fix. R1's M4 (allowlist/email) was already dismissed as subsumed by the BYO pivot; this is its cousin. Keep the existing "verify at M2 on a fresh dev app" impl-note (Spotify policy churn is real), which the milestone already implies. No onboarding self-add step is added.

M3: UPHELD (Must-fix) - Translocation is real (fact 4) and the defense's "the documented flow clears it" is context-blind in the opposite direction: "drag to /Applications" is one line on a download page, and launching straight from the mounted DMG is among the most common real-world macOS user behaviors, not a mis-install edge case. The failure it produces is precisely the severity bar's worst shape: onboarding *appears to succeed* from the translocated path, the login item is registered against a randomized path that won't exist at next login, and the app silently never launches again — permanent, invisible death of the ambient promise on a common path. The fix is the prosecution's own guard and it is cheap (detect translocated/not-in-/Applications at first launch; require the move *before* requesting FDA or registering the login item — LetsMove-style). Cheap fix does not lower severity; silent-death-on-common-path is what Must-fix is for.

M4: UPHELD (Must-fix) - Effectively conceded by the defense, and correctly so. `SMAppService` registration can land `.requiresApproval`, macOS notifies the user of new login items with a live toggle, and a user can disable it in Settings at any time. If status is not `.enabled`, the "never open again" promise fails silently at next login — squarely the severity bar. The fix is small and rides existing machinery: check `SMAppService.status` on every launch and periodically, and surface "Enable Friend List in Login Items" (with the `SMAppService.openSystemSettingsLoginItems()` deep link) through the M6 attention-state surface; add an M4 acceptance line. Small fix, real Must-fix — it guards the same launch-persistence leg M3 attacks.

M5: DOWNGRADED (Low — unchanged from R1-M5) - Pure re-litigation. R1 explicitly downgraded this to Low ("the user is present at the screen… not silent background failure"), and R2 re-raises it to Must-fix with zero new reasoning — the "try again / can't continue" copy and installed-but-paused state it asks for are the same stuck-state copy R1 scoped as Low (now also tracked by R2's own L5 timeout nudge). Nothing new to adjudicate; R1's verdict stands.

M6: DOWNGRADED (Low — unchanged from R1-M7) - Pure re-litigation. R1 downgraded this to Low: the retry queue's chat/sender fields never leave the Mac, so the privacy *pillar* is intact and the residue is a one-line wording mismatch (drop the two fields from the retry queue, or amend the §Privacy sentence). R2 restates the identical content with no new argument. R1's verdict stands.

M7: DOWNGRADED (Medium) - Genuinely new (a real C2/BYO side-effect) and factually right: Premium and create-your-own-dev-app are disclosed on screen 3, after the invasive FDA grant on screen 2, so a non-Premium user grants the scariest permission before learning the app can never work for them. But it does not meet the Must-fix bar: for the supported (Premium) user the core promise is untouched, and for the non-Premium user the failure is neither silent nor unrecoverable — they are present, informed, and the grant is reversible. What it damages is the trust pillar of a privacy-first product, which is exactly why the fix should be done anyway: it is pure copy re-ordering (state "requires Spotify Premium; you'll create your own free Spotify app — takes ~2 minutes" on Welcome, before the FDA ask). Medium, with a strong recommendation to just do it — it is nearly free.

## Holistic Assessment

Surviving set: **M3 (Must-fix), M4 (Must-fix)**; Medium C1(→R1-M8), M1, M7; Low M5(=R1), M6(=R1).

R2's headline claims — a Critical that "kills the app" and a Must-fix that breaks BYO auth — are both built on false platform premises and fall. What actually survives is more interesting than either side's framing: **every upheld or downgraded R2 item clusters on a single seam the R1 review never stress-tested — whether the app reliably *keeps existing* across quits, crashes, and logins.** M3 (translocated path → login item registered against a path that evaporates), M4 (login item declined/toggled off), and M1 (crash with no restart until login) all attack launch persistence; C1/M8 attacks surviving the one relaunch that onboarding itself commonly provokes.

Co-occurrence is where it turns severe. Any one of M3/M4 alone silently kills auto-launch. M1 *combined with* M4 is worse than either verdict reads in isolation: a crash normally costs only "until next login" — but if the login item is unapproved or toggled off, there is no next login recovery, and a single crash becomes permanent invisible product death. As in R1's M2+M6 pairing, these deserve treatment as **one work item — "launch survivability":** (1) the M3 install-location guard *before* FDA/login-item registration, (2) the M4 `SMAppService.status` check wired into the existing attention surface, (3) the M1 documented recovery decision (accept login-only in writing, or the same-executable KeepAlive watchdog), and (4) M8's onboarding-step persistence + LSUIElement window re-presentation. All four are small; together they close the seam. An M4/M5 milestone acceptance line should read: *"kill -9 the app, toggle the login item off and on, reboot — scanning resumes with no user action beyond the attention prompt."*

With the two Must-fixes applied (plus the Medium set, all cheap), the app still delivers its core promise; nothing in R2 disturbs the architecture R1 settled. **Verdict on the plan: the R1-fixed architecture remains sound; R2 contributes no new architectural defect but exposes launch survivability as the remaining weak seam — apply M3+M4 as prerequisites before M5 (ship), fold the Mediums into M3/M4 milestone acceptance.**

One flag outside the adjudicated list, endorsed from the defense (which the prosecution missed): community reports that Spotify's 2025–26 Development-Mode tightening may restrict **playlist write** for dev-mode apps. Unverified here, but if true it kills the core promise regardless of everything above. The very first action of milestone M2 should be a live `POST /v1/playlists/{id}/tracks` from a fresh dev-mode app — before any further Spotify work is built.
